CHAPTER I
This chapter includes the introduction, statement of the problem, objectives of the study, conceptual framework, significance of the study, scope and limitations, and definition of terms. It discusses the inventory management practices and challenges of the Supply and Property Management Unit of the University of Science and Technology of the Southern Philippines and presents the proposed Design and Development of a Web-Based Supply and Property Management System with Integrated Inventory and Consumable Release Tracking to improve inventory monitoring, reporting, and operational efficiency.

Introduction
This chapter presents the background and context of the study, emphasizing the importance of efficient supply and property management in educational institutions and the challenges associated with manual and spreadsheet-based inventory processes. It discusses the existing inventory and consumable supply management practices within the University of Science and Technology of the Southern Philippines (USTP), including the monitoring and release of consumable supplies handled by the Supply and Property Management Unit. The chapter also highlights issues such as inaccurate inventory tracking, delays in the release of supplies, duplication of records, and difficulties in generating timely reports caused by traditional manual systems. Furthermore, it identifies the limitations of the current inventory management approach and establishes the need for a computerized and centralized system. Finally, the chapter introduces the proposed Web-Based Supply and Property Management System with Integrated Inventory and Consumable Release Tracking as a solution to improve operational efficiency, inventory accuracy, real-time monitoring, report generation, and decision-making within the university. 

The continuous advancement of technology has transformed the way organizations manage records, inventory, and operational processes. Many institutions now adopt computerized inventory management systems to improve efficiency, accuracy, transparency, and real-time monitoring of supplies and properties. Modern inventory systems provide automated tracking, centralized databases, and report generation features that help reduce manual errors and improve decision-making processes (Martinez et al., 2024).
Inventory management plays an important role in educational institutions because it ensures the proper recording, monitoring, storage, and distribution of institutional resources. Effective inventory systems help maintain accurate records of supplies, equipment, and consumable items necessary for academic and administrative operations. According to Santos et al. (2021), automated inventory systems improve inventory tracking efficiency and minimize document loss compared to manual processes.
Despite technological advancements, many institutions still rely on manual and spreadsheet-based methods in monitoring consumable supplies and inventory transactions. These traditional methods often result in inaccurate tracking, delays in supply release, duplication of records, and difficulty in generating reports. Reyes and Cruz (2020) emphasized that manual inventory systems increase the risk of human error and inefficient resource monitoring.
At the University of Science and Technology of the Southern Philippines (USTP), the Supply and Property Management Unit is responsible for managing campus inventory, monitoring supplies, and facilitating the release of consumable items to faculty and staff. Although the university currently utilizes the General Inventory and Asset Management System (GIAMS) for certain inventory functions, the system does not fully support the release and tracking of consumable supplies.
To address these challenges, this study proposes the design and development of a Web-Based Supply and Property Management System with Integrated Inventory and Consumable Release Tracking for USTP. The proposed system aims to automate inventory monitoring, improve consumable release tracking, generate accurate reports, and enhance operational efficiency within the university.

Statement of the Problem
The Supply and Property Management Unit of the University of Science and Technology of the Southern Philippines (USTP) experiences significant challenges in the release and tracking of consumable supplies due to the continued use of manual and spreadsheet-based inventory processes. Although the university utilizes the General Inventory and Asset Management System (GIAMS) for certain inventory functions, the system does not fully support the monitoring and tracking of consumable supply releases. These challenges affect the accuracy, efficiency, and reliability of inventory management operations within the university.
Inaccurate Tracking of Consumable Supplies
The manual recording and monitoring of consumable supplies often result in discrepancies between the actual stock and recorded inventory data. This leads to inaccurate inventory records, making it difficult for personnel to determine the exact availability of supplies and monitor inventory movement effectively.
Delays in the Release of Supplies
The current manual process in approving and releasing consumable supplies causes delays in transactions and processing time. The absence of an automated tracking system slows down the release procedure and affects the timely availability of supplies needed by faculty and staff.
Duplication and Mismatch of Records
Manual and spreadsheet-based inventory monitoring increases the possibility of duplicated entries and mismatched records. These inconsistencies create confusion in tracking released items and affect the reliability of inventory reports and stock monitoring.
Difficulty in Monitoring Incoming and Outgoing Supplies
The existing system lacks real-time monitoring of incoming and outgoing consumable supplies. As a result, supply personnel experience difficulty in tracking stock movement, monitoring item availability, and identifying shortages or overstocked items promptly.
Delayed Generation of Reports
The manual preparation of inventory documents such as Requisition and Issue Slips (RIS), stock cards, and inventory reports consumes significant time and effort. This results in delays in generating accurate and updated reports needed for monitoring and decision-making.
High Risk of Human Error
The dependence on manual data entry and physical recording increases the likelihood of human errors such as incorrect encoding, missing records, and inaccurate categorization of supplies. These errors negatively affect inventory accuracy and operational efficiency.

Lack of Centralized Inventory Monitoring
The absence of a centralized and integrated system makes inventory monitoring and consumable release tracking difficult to manage efficiently. Information is scattered across different files and spreadsheets, making retrieval and updating of records time-consuming and less reliable.

 Objectives of the Study
The main objective of this study is to design and develop a Web-Based Supply and Property Management System with Integrated Inventory and Consumable Release Tracking for the University of Science and Technology of the Southern Philippines (USTP) capable of efficiently recording, monitoring, tracking, and managing inventory data for consumable supplies, office materials, and other institutional resources. The proposed system aims to provide accurate and real-time inventory information to improve consumable supply monitoring, reduce delays in supply release, minimize mismatched records and human errors, and support faster decision-making within the Supply and Property Management Unit. This initiative seeks to provide USTP with a reliable, organized, and automated inventory management process that promotes transparency, accountability, and operational efficiency.
Specifically, this study aims to:
Develop a Web-Based Supply and Property Management System capable of recording, tracking, and managing inventory items, consumable supplies, and supply transactions through a centralized database system.
Implement an inventory and consumable release monitoring feature that provides real-time updates on stock levels, item availability, supply issuance, and replenishment to minimize delays in release and mismatched inventory records.
Store and maintain inventory and consumable release records electronically to improve data security, reduce the risk of missing records, and allow easier retrieval of information by authorized personnel.
Generate accurate and timely reports such as Requisition and Issue Slips (RIS), stock cards, inventory summaries, and consumable release reports to support effective monitoring and decision-making within the Supply and Property Management Unit.
Evaluate the developed system in terms of usability, functionality, efficiency, reliability, and security based on ISO/IEC 25010 standards to determine its effectiveness in meeting the operational needs of the Supply and Property Management Unit of USTP.

Scope and Limitations
This study focuses on the design and development of a Web-Based Supply and Property Management System with Integrated Inventory and Consumable Release Tracking for the University of Science and Technology of the Southern Philippines (USTP). The system includes inventory management, consumable release tracking, monitoring of incoming and outgoing supplies, RIS and stock card generation, report generation, and user management. The system will be deployed locally within the USTP campus network as a Progressive Web Application (PWA) accessible to authorized personnel. Integration with external suppliers, online procurement systems, barcode or RFID technologies, other university information systems, and advanced asset lifecycle management features are beyond the scope of the study. The system also depends on the accuracy of data entered by authorized users.

Figure 3.1: Dashboard

The dashboard serves as the main interface displayed after the user successfully logs into the system. It provides a comprehensive overview of the current supply and property status and recent system activities.
The dashboard displays four (4) key metric cards at the top of the interface, showing the total number of inventory items, the count of low stock items that have fallen below the reorder level, the number of deliveries received for the current month, and the total number of Release of Inventory Slips (RIS) processed for the current period. These metric cards allow authorized users to immediately assess the operational status of the supply office at a glance.
Below the metric cards, the dashboard presents two (2) side-by-side panels. The left panel displays a horizontal bar chart showing the stock quantity distributed across all item categories such as Office Supplies, Janitorial, IT Supplies, Medical, and Electrical supplies, allowing users to visually compare stock levels per category. The right panel shows a list of pending supply requests submitted by Faculty and Staff end users, displaying the item name, requesting department, quantity, and current approval status. Through this interface, users can quickly navigate to different system modules such as Inventory, Deliveries, Releases (RIS), Reports, Manage Users, Roles, System Settings, and System Logs using the navigation menu located on the left side of the screen. At the bottom, a full-width low stock items table lists all items that have reached or fallen below their reorder level, including the item code, item name, category, unit, quantity on hand, reorder level, and status indicator. The dashboard helps improve inventory monitoring, supports faster decision-making, and provides organized access to all supply and property-related information.

Figure 3.2: Inventory Management
In Figure 3.2, the Inventory Management Interface of the proposed system is presented. This page is accessible to the Supply and Property Officer and serves as the central module for managing all inventory items recorded in the system.
At the top of the interface, three (3) summary metric cards display the total number of items across all categories, the number of items currently below the reorder level, and the total number of active item categories. Below the metric cards, the main inventory table is displayed within a panel that includes a search bar for finding specific items by name or code, and a category dropdown filter to narrow down the displayed records by item classification.
The inventory table contains the following columns: Item Code, Item Name, Category, Unit of Measurement, Quantity on Hand, Reorder Level, Status, and an Action column. The Status column uses color-coded badges to indicate the stock condition of each item "OK" displayed in green for items with sufficient stock, "Low" displayed in amber for items approaching the reorder threshold, and "Critical" displayed in red for items that have already fallen below the reorder level. An "Add Item" button is provided in the upper right of the panel, allowing the Supply Officer to register new inventory items into the system. Each row in the table has an edit icon that allows the officer to update item details. A pagination control at the bottom of the table allows the user to navigate through multiple pages of inventory records.

Figure 3.3: Record Deliveries
In Figure 3.3, the Record Deliveries Interface of the proposed system is presented. This page allows the Supply and Property Officer to record incoming supplies delivered by external suppliers, which automatically updates the inventory stock levels upon saving.
The interface is divided into two (2) panels arranged side by side. The left panel contains the new delivery entry form, which includes input fields for the Delivery Reference Number, Date Delivered, Supplier Name, and Official Receipt or Purchase Order Number. A dropdown field allows the officer to select the name of the personnel who received the delivery. Below the header fields, a dynamic item entry section allows the officer to add multiple items to a single delivery record by selecting the item name, specifying the quantity received, and entering the unit cost. Added items are displayed in a summary table showing the item name, quantity, unit cost, and computed total cost. The cumulative total cost of all delivery items is displayed at the bottom of the form. A "Save Delivery" button finalizes and records the transaction into the database.
The right panel displays a list of recent delivery records, showing the Delivery Reference Number, Supplier Name, Date, and the number of items included in each delivery. This allows the officer to quickly review previously recorded deliveries without leaving the current page.

Figure 3.4: Process Consumable Release / RIS
In Figure 3.4, the Process Consumable Release Interface, also referred to as the Requisition and Issue Slip (RIS) Module, is presented. This page is used by the Supply and Property Officer to process the release of consumable supplies to requesting departments or offices.
The interface is organized into two (2) panels. The left panel contains the RIS entry form, which includes fields for the RIS Number, Release Date, Requesting Department, and SCIO (School or Campus/Organizational Unit). A text area is provided for the officer to encode the stated purpose of the release. Below the header information, an item selection section allows the officer to specify the items to be released and the corresponding quantities. As items are added, they appear in a summary table that shows the item name, quantity requested, and current stock on hand. Items with critically low stock are flagged with a color-coded badge directly within the table to alert the officer before confirming the release. Action buttons at the bottom of the form allow the officer to either save the record as a draft or submit it for approval.
The right panel presents the RIS history table, which lists previously submitted release slips with columns for RIS Number, Department, Date, and Status. A status filter dropdown and an export button are available above the table, allowing the officer to filter records by approval status and download reports as needed.

Figure 3.5 Reports
In Figure 3.5, the Reports Generation Interface of the proposed system is presented. This page provides the Supply and Property Officer with the ability to generate, view, and export various official reports required by the General Services Office.
At the top of the interface, five (5) report type cards are displayed in a horizontal grid, each representing a specific report that the system can produce. These include the Stock Card, which serves as a per-item ledger of all receipts and issuances; the RIS / Release Form, which generates a printable copy of a specific release slip; the Monthly / Quarterly / Yearly Reports, which summarize supply activities over a selected time period; the Per SCIO Report, which consolidates supply data by campus or organizational unit; and the Transaction History Per Item, which provides a complete movement history for a selected inventory item.
The currently selected report type is highlighted with a distinct border to indicate the active report being previewed. Below the report type selector, a report preview panel displays a sample output of the selected report. In the case of the Stock Card, the preview shows the item details at the top including the item code, unit of measure, and reorder level, followed by a tabular ledger containing columns for Date, Reference Number, Receipt Quantity, Issue Quantity, and Running Balance. Print and Export buttons are provided in the upper right of the preview panel, allowing the officer to produce a hard copy or download the report in the desired file format.

Figure 3.6: Request Supplies(Faculty and Staff — End User View)
In Figure 3.6, the Request Supplies Interface for Faculty and Staff end users is presented. This page is specifically designed for non-administrative users who need to submit requests for consumable supplies from the supply office.
The interface is divided into two (2) panels. The left panel contains the supply request form, which includes a dropdown field for selecting the requesting department, a date field for specifying when the supplies are needed, and a text area for stating the purpose of the request. An item selection section below allows the requestor to choose items from the available inventory and specify the quantity needed for each. Selected items are listed in a summary table showing the item name and quantity. A "Submit Request" button at the bottom of the form sends the request to the Supply and Property Officer for review and approval.
The right panel is divided into two sections. The upper section shows a tabular list of all previous requests submitted by the logged-in user, displaying the Reference Number, Date, Number of Items, and Approval Status. The lower section shows the detailed information of the currently selected request, including the approval status, the name of the approving officer, the date of approval, and any remarks provided by the supply office. This allows end users to track the real-time status of all their supply requests without needing to contact the supply office directly.


Figure 3.7: Manage Users
In Figure 3.7, the Manage Users Interface of the proposed system is presented. This page is exclusively accessible to the System Administrator and is used to create, update, and manage the user accounts of all personnel who have access to the system.
At the top of the user list panel, a search bar and a role filter dropdown allow the administrator to locate specific user accounts quickly. An "Add User" button in the upper right opens a form for registering a new system user. The main content area displays a user table with columns for a user avatar or initials, Full Name, Email Address, Assigned Role, SCIO or Department, Account Status, and an Action column. Each user's assigned role is displayed using a color-coded badge to visually distinguish between Admins, Supply Officers, Faculty, and Staff. The account status column uses a green "Active" badge for currently active accounts and a red "Inactive" badge for deactivated accounts. The edit icon in the Action column allows the administrator to modify user information, reset passwords, change roles, or deactivate accounts as necessary.

Figure 3.8: Manage Roles
In Figure 3.8, the Manage Roles Interface of the proposed system is presented. This page allows the System Administrator to define and configure the roles used within the system, as well as set the specific permissions associated with each role.
The interface is divided into two (2) panels. The left panel displays the list of currently defined roles in the system, including Admin, Supply Officer, and Faculty/Staff. Each role card shows the role name, a brief description of its access level, and the number of users currently assigned to that role. The currently selected role is highlighted with a distinct border. A "New Role" button at the top of the panel allows the administrator to create additional roles if required.
The right panel displays the permissions matrix for the selected role. Each system feature or module is listed as a row, accompanied by a toggle switch that the administrator can enable or disable to grant or restrict access. The permission items include functions such as managing inventory, recording deliveries, processing RIS, generating reports, managing users, configuring system settings, viewing system logs, and approving supply requests. A "Save Permissions" button at the bottom of the panel commits all changes made to the role's permission configuration.

Figure 3.9: System Settings
In Figure 3.9, the System Settings Interface of the proposed system is presented. This page is accessible only to the System Administrator and provides configuration options for the general operational parameters of the system.
The interface is organized into two (2) columns. The left column contains a General Settings form that allows the administrator to define institution-level configurations, including the Institution Name, System Title, Fiscal Year Start month, Currency used in financial computations, and the Low Stock Notification Threshold expressed as a percentage of the reorder level. A "Save Changes" button at the bottom of the form applies all modifications to the system configuration.
The right column is further divided into two (2) sub-panels. The upper sub-panel manages the list of item categories used throughout the system, displaying each category name and its active or inactive status, with an edit icon for modifying existing entries and an "Add" button for creating new categories. The lower sub-panel manages the units of measurement used when encoding inventory items, displaying the unit name and its standard abbreviation, with similar add and edit functionality. These settings ensure that the system's reference data remains accurate and consistent with the operational standards of the General Services Office.

Figure 3.10:  System Logs
In Figure 3.10, the System Logs Interface, also referred to as the Audit Trail Module, is presented. This page is exclusively accessible to the System Administrator and provides a complete chronological record of all actions performed by users within the system.
At the top of the interface, a search bar allows the administrator to look up specific log entries by keyword, while two (2) dropdown filters enable filtering of records by Action Type  such as Login, Create, Update, and Delete and by the User who performed the action. An "Export" button allows the administrator to download the log records for documentation and audit purposes.
The main content area presents the audit trail in a tabular format with the following columns: Timestamp, which records the exact date and time the action was performed; User, identifying the account that performed the action; Action, displayed as a color-coded badge indicating the type of operation; Module, identifying which part of the system was affected; and Description, providing a plain-language summary of the specific action taken. A pagination control at the bottom of the table allows the administrator to navigate through the complete history of system activity. This module ensures accountability and traceability of all transactions and administrative actions performed within the Supply and Property Management System.
